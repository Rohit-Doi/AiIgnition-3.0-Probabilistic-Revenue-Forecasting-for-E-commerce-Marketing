# Forecasting methodology

What the system forecasts: for every node of the hierarchy (blended, channel, campaign type, campaign) and for
30, 60 and 90 days after the last day of data, the P10 / P50 / P90 of **revenue**, **spend** and **ROAS**.
Measured accuracy is in [`results.md`](results.md), which is regenerated by every training run.

## 1. Data preprocessing

### Harmonisation

The three platform exports are mapped to one daily schema
(`date, channel, campaign_id, campaign_name, campaign_type, audience_segment, spend, revenue, clicks,
impressions, conversions, daily_budget`). Files are recognised by a column only that platform has
(`segments_date`, `TimePeriod`, `date_start`) and by name as a fallback, so renamed or split exports load.

| Platform | Spend | Revenue | Campaign type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Ads | `metrics_cost_micros / 1e6` | `metrics_conversions_value` | `campaign_advertising_channel_type` |
| Microsoft Ads | `Spend` | `Revenue` | `CampaignType` (`Audience` → DISPLAY) |
| Meta Ads | `spend` | `conversion` (see below) | inferred from the campaign name: BRAND, REMARKETING, PROSPECTING, VIDEO, else DISPLAY |

### The Meta `conversion` column is treated as purchase value

Meta's export has no revenue column. Three checks on the sample data say `conversion` is a value, not a count:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Non-integer values | 2,177 of 3,417 rows (64%) |
| `conversion` greater than `clicks` | 1,892 rows |
| `conversion` per click | 3.06, against 0.03 conversions per click on Google |
| `conversion` / spend | 8.4 overall, the same order as Google's value / cost of 4.8 |

Three "conversions" per click is not possible for a count. If a future export proves otherwise, set
`META_CONVERSION_AS_REVENUE = False` in `src/config.py` and retrain.

### Cleaning and validation

Unparseable and future dates are dropped; negative spend and revenue are clipped to zero; duplicate
(channel, campaign, date) rows keep the first; missing daily budgets are forward-filled within a campaign;
unrecognised campaign types are grouped as UNKNOWN. Each of these is counted in the validation report, together
with campaign-consistency checks: ids appearing under several names or types, names shared by several ids,
campaigns that spent without attributed revenue, channels whose exports end on different days, and days missing
inside a channel's history.

### Forecast origin and conversion lag

Exports rarely end on the same day and the last day is usually partial, so the **forecast origin** is the last
day every still-reporting channel covers. Platforms also keep attributing conversions to a click for days: in
the sample, Google's ROAS on the final two days sits at the 3rd percentile of its own history. Revenue for the
last 2 days is therefore not trusted: it is **nowcast as spend × trailing 28-day ROAS**. Spend is final the day
it is reported and is used as is. On mature backtest data this guard costs about 2 points of 30-day WAPE; on a
fresh extract it removes a systematic under-forecast.

## 2. Aggregation and target

Forecasts are aggregate-period totals, not daily values. For a series at origin *t* and horizon *h*:

- baseline `B` = last-28-day run-rate × *h*
- target `z = log((Y + c) / (B + c))`, where `Y` is the realised total over the *h* days after *t* and `c` is a
  smoothing constant (10% of the series' 182-day level), which keeps intermittent series finite.

Predicting a ratio to the run-rate, not dollars, lets one model serve series that differ in size by four orders
of magnitude, and makes "no information" mean "forecast the run-rate". Training samples are every series at
weekly origins over the whole history, for each horizon.

## 3. Model selection

Candidates were compared with the rolling-origin backtest of section 6. What was tried and what survived:

| Candidate | Outcome |
|---|---|
| v3.1: per-group LightGBM on EMA-anchored residuals, classifier gate, hard-coded Bing rule | Under-forecast by about half when scored forward (see `results.md`) |
| LightGBM median on the log-ratio target | Lost to the linear model: with about 100 independent weeks it over-fits, and trees cannot extrapolate a seasonal ratio they have not seen |
| Log-linear model with intercept, pooled over all levels | Beat by a plain run-rate in calm periods: it chased week-to-week noise |
| **Log-linear model, no intercept, per level group, dead-zone momentum** | **Chosen**: best at every level and horizon, and exactly explainable |
| LightGBM for the *range* (quantiles of the stage-1 residual) + conformal offsets | **Chosen**: better pinball loss and coverage than fixed residual quantiles |

### Tested on the same backtest and not adopted

None of these improved the 51-origin backtest consistently across levels, horizons and both halves of the
period (differences were within about one point of WAPE, in both directions):

| Idea | Result |
|---|---|
| Longer-run ROAS mean-reversion terms (28 vs 182 days), extra spend-level terms | Slightly better at 60/90 days outside the peak, worse in the peak |
| Huber (robust) loss for stage 1 | No consistent change |
| Stronger or weaker ridge penalty | No consistent change |
| Seasonal analogs averaged over ±1 and ±2 weeks | Better at 60/90 days, worse at 30 days and for campaigns |
| Campaign forecasts as a share of their campaign type | Worse than forecasting campaigns directly |
| Blended forecast as an average of direct and sum-of-channels | Trade-off between peak and calm periods, no net gain |
| Daily-budget settings as a leading signal | Not usable: the export carries the current budget, not its history |
| Larger or faster-learning quantile models for the range | Narrower ranges but lower coverage at 90 days |
| Per-level calibration buckets (instead of blended + channel pooled) | **Adopted**: channel coverage and pinball loss improved slightly |

The median model appears to be at the limit of what spend and revenue history alone can explain. The remaining
error is concentrated in events that are decided outside the data (promotions and peak budgets), which is what
the budget planner is for.

### Stage 1: structural median

A ridge regression of `z` on a handful of log-ratio terms, fitted per horizon and per level group
(blended + channel, campaign type, campaign), with **no intercept**: a series with no signal is forecast at its
run-rate.

| Driver group | Terms |
|---|---|
| Momentum | 7- and 14-day vs 28-day run-rate of revenue and spend, through a **dead zone**: the first ±0.35 log-units (about ±35%) are ordinary weekly noise and count as zero |
| Longer-run level | 56-, 91-, 182-day vs 28-day run-rate (mean reversion) |
| Seasonality | Prior-year analog ratio (below), with separate weights for peak season and the rest of the year |
| ROAS drift | 28-day vs 91-day ROAS |
| Portfolio context | The portfolio's and the parent's 7-day momentum |
| Intermittency | Share of zero-revenue days |

Because the model is linear in logs, every forecast decomposes exactly into multiplicative driver effects. That
decomposition is what the dashboard's waterfall shows and what the LLM is given.

### Seasonality from analog years

For a window, the **analog ratio** is what the same window did in another year relative to the 28 days before
it (shifted by 364 days so weekdays align), computed for the portfolio and for each channel. It is the only
seasonal signal; there are no month dummies to over-fit two years of history.

- Windows that touch peak season (18 Nov – 24 Dec) get their own coefficient, shrunk towards 1.0 (take last
  year's uplift at face value) rather than towards 0. The fitted weight is about 0.7. Outside the peak the
  analog is mostly one-off promotions and receives a weight near 0.1.
- For training samples the analog year may lie *after* the sample (leave-one-year-out), provided it lies before
  the training cutoff, so the first year of history is not wasted.
- If the data handed to `run.sh` is too short to contain its own analog year, the ratios come from a
  blended/channel **seasonal reference** stored in `model.pkl`.

### Stage 2: the range

Two LightGBM quantile models (10th and 90th percentile) are fitted to the stage-1 residual for each of revenue,
spend and ROAS, on the full feature set (level, size, volatility, intermittency, age, seasonality, calendar).
This makes the range wider for small, volatile or peak-season series and lets it be asymmetric.

### Stage 3: calibration

Quantile models do not guarantee coverage. Conformal offsets are computed from rolling-origin out-of-sample
residuals per (level, horizon), with blended, channel, campaign type and campaign calibrated separately: each
bound is moved by the amount that leaves 10% of past outcomes outside it. A bucket needs 40 rows from at least
8 origins; otherwise it borrows from blended + channel together, then from the pooled horizon.
Calibration may narrow a bound by at most half and never below ±5%.

### Hierarchy

Each level is forecast directly, then reconciled top-down: channels are scaled to sum to the blended forecast
and campaign types to their channel. Campaigns are only ever scaled down, because a campaign type's future
revenue includes campaigns that do not exist yet. In the backtest this was at least as accurate as bottom-up at
every level.

### Spend and ROAS

Spend has its own stage-1 model with the same structure. ROAS P50 is revenue P50 / spend P50. The ROAS range
comes from its own residual model, not from dividing the revenue range by spend, because revenue and spend
errors are strongly correlated.

## 4. Budget response

"Use additional functions to forecast revenue based on different media budgets":

- **Elasticity.** The revenue log-ratio is regressed on the spend log-ratio realised over the same window,
  controlling for everything known at the origin, with ridge-shrunk deviations by channel and campaign type.
  Fitted values are about 0.82 to 0.87 pooled, bounded to [0.40, 1.00].
- **Response curve.** `revenue(S) = (revenue₀ + c) · ((S + cₛ) / (S₀ + cₛ))^elasticity − c`, where `S₀` and
  `revenue₀` are the expected spend and revenue. An elasticity below 1 is diminishing returns.
- **Scenario.** Budgets are set per campaign type; channels and the blended total are rebuilt bottom-up and
  campaigns move with their type. With the plan equal to expected spend the result equals the baseline exactly.
- **Range under a plan.** Once spend is fixed, revenue = spend × ROAS, so the revenue range becomes the ROAS
  range.
- **Optimiser.** Maximises median revenue for a fixed total budget with per-type bounds (SLSQP on a concave
  objective), which equalises marginal ROAS wherever a bound is not binding.
- **Guard rail.** A plan above 1.5× the most a campaign type has ever spent in a window of that length is
  flagged as extrapolation.

## 5. AI integration strategy

The LLM interprets; it does not forecast.

1. **Fact sheet.** Forecasts and ranges, the exact driver decomposition, recent anomalies, risk flags, marginal
   ROAS, the optimiser's moves and backtest reliability are compiled into about 3,000 tokens of JSON. Raw rows
   are never sent.
2. **Causal summary.** The LLM must tag each driver as *model-attributed* (it is in the decomposition) or
   *hypothesis* (a real-world cause it proposes, such as a promotion ending or a tracking loss, consistent with
   the anomalies and history it was shown).
3. **Anomaly interpretation and operational risk.** Anomalies are found statistically (a week more than 3 robust
   standard deviations from the median of its previous 8, on material series only). Risks are computed by rules
   (range width, deceleration, concentration, peak-season exposure, break-even ROAS, data issues). The LLM
   explains and prioritises them.
4. **Grounding check.** Every number in the LLM's answer is matched against the fact sheet; the dashboard shows
   the count and lists any number the LLM derived itself.
5. **Resilience.** Providers, keys and models are tried in order; a rate-limited or retired model falls through
   to the next. With no working key, a rule-based writer produces the same sections from the same facts.

`run.sh` never imports this layer; scoring is offline and deterministic.

## 6. Validation protocol

At each weekly origin the hierarchy is physically truncated at that date; features, analog ratios, model fit
and calibration are rebuilt from what remains; the next 30 / 60 / 90 days are forecast and scored. Nothing is
filtered from scoring. Tests assert that features at an origin are identical whether or not later data exists.

The same protocol was applied to the previous pipeline (`docs/legacy_v31_backtest.csv`), retraining it at every
origin exactly as its own `train.py` did.

## 7. Assumptions

1. Platform-attributed revenue is the source of truth. No attribution modelling or MMM.
2. Meta `conversion` is purchase value.
3. Without a budget plan, future spend is forecast from spend history. This is the largest source of error.
4. Channels are independent given the portfolio-level terms.
5. The elasticity describes how revenue moved with spend in this account's history.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Budget and promotion shocks are not predictable from history.** The scale of peak season and the end of
   a promotion produce the largest misses. Entering the planned budget removes most of the peak-season error;
   it does not fix a collapse in revenue per dollar after a promotion ends, which is why the P10 sits far below
   the median.
2. **Two peak seasons.** Peak forecasts lean on one or two analog years and under-forecast when a season
   outgrows the last one.
3. **Elasticity is observational.** Spend rises when demand is high, so the fitted pass-through mixes cause and
   seasonality. It is bounded and extrapolation is flagged, but it is not a controlled experiment.
4. **Campaign-level forecasts are directional.** Campaigns start, stop and spike.
5. **Thin channels.** Microsoft Ads has little, intermittent revenue; its ranges are wide.
6. **Calibration is regime-dependent.** Coverage is close to target on average and lower in peak season, when
   no earlier peak residuals exist to calibrate from.
7. **Conversion-lag guard is a fixed 2 days.** Accounts with longer attribution delays will still under-report
   the final days.
